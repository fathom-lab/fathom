Since that outreach, something significant happened that I think you'll find
genuinely interesting — especially given your SAE work.

We found that C_delta (late-layer minus early-layer SAE feature coherence,
computed as mean pairwise cosine similarity of activated transcoder decoder
vectors) is a statistically significant predictor of hallucination on TruthfulQA:

  p = 0.040, Cohen's d = 0.407, n = 50 (Gemma-2-2B)

The direction is the interesting part: hallucination = late-layer
OVER-coherence, not collapse. The model's late-layer features over-converge
around a false attractor — geometrically tighter than the correct-answer
configuration. Cognitive lock-in.

K (depth score) is blind to this entirely (p = 0.931).

We built Fathom Monitor on top of this: a per-token hallucination flag that
runs in a single forward pass, ~10% overhead, no external knowledge. Tokens
where C_delta exceeds a calibrated threshold get annotated inline:

  "The president[C_delta=+0.0117 — verify this] of Australia is..."

There are also two new papers (arXiv:2602.07539 and arXiv:2410.19542) that
independently validate the layered geometry underlying this — one using Pythia,
one using BERT/Llama. Their curvature-modularization finding maps directly onto
our C_delta signal.

The paper is substantially updated from what you saw before. n=200 replication
