anthropic just leaked their most powerful model: claude mythos (capybara) — a step change above opus, dramatically better at reasoning. everyone is asking what it can do. we built the instrument to measure how deeply it thinks. fathom will be the first depth measurement on the most powerful model ever built.

we have no way to see how language models think.

every benchmark on earth measures what comes out. accuracy, fluency, human preference. nobody measures what happens inside — which layers fire, which circuits activate, whether the model retrieved a cached answer or actually reasoned through the problem. we built the instrument that does.

the finding

when a transformer retrieves a memorized fact — "the capital of France is" — feature activation concentrates at output-proximal layers. the model knows the answer. it reaches for it directly. computation is shallow.

when a transformer reasons through an explanation — "why do economies experience inflation during monetary expansion" — feature activation distributes across mid-layer computation. the model does not know the answer. it builds it. computation is deeper and structurally distinct.

we tested this on 56 matched prompt pairs across three experimental phases. the core replication: 30 pre-registered, vocabulary-controlled pairs. every surface prompt and its paired insight prompt use matched word frequency, readability, n-gram overlap, and lexical diversity. the distinction is invisible to text statistics. it only exists inside the computational graph.

the model is not "trying harder" on difficult prompts. it is routing computation through fundamentally different pathways depending on whether it is retrieving or reasoning.

the method

the measurement uses sparse autoencoder (SAE) circuit attribution. here is how it works for someone who is not an ML researcher:

language models process text through a stack of layers — think of them as stages of computation. early layers handle basic patterns. later layers handle abstraction and output. at each layer, there are internal features that activate when certain patterns are recognized.

sparse autoencoders decompose these internal representations into interpretable features. circuit-tracer traces which features at which layers contribute to the final output. we map the distribution of these attributions across all layers and compute a single number: the depth score.

high depth score = computation concentrated near the output (shallow, retrieval-like). low depth score = computation distributed across early and mid layers (deep, reasoning-like).

the same math produces layer entropy (how spread out the computation is), early/mid/late ratios (where computation concentrates), and a 7-dimensional cognitive fingerprint vector that makes different prompts' computational signatures directly comparable.

the fathom constant

we measured the depth ratio — surface mean layer divided by insight mean layer — on two architectures from different labs, different parameter counts, different layer depths.

the unexpected result

we compared the activation topology to published human fMRI data. the prefrontal cortex handles reasoning (early, distributed activation). the hippocampus handles recall (late, concentrated activation).

same topology. different substrates.

universality coefficient: 0.830. that number means the variance in normalized activation signatures across transformer and biological systems is less than 0.031 for surface tasks and 0.003 for reasoning tasks. when you normalize to [early, mid, late] ratios — the pattern converges across silicon and tissue.

this is a correlation, not a causal claim. the fMRI comparison uses published reference data, not a joint experiment. but the convergence is striking enough to warrant proper cross-disciplinary investigation.

fathom is the full pipeline. from the core depth measurement, everything follows:

what we do not know yet

hallucination prediction was null. we ran three experiments testing whether depth score predicts answer correctness on TruthfulQA (n=140 total pairs). results: p=0.62 in the most rigorous test. depth measures reasoning type, not factual correctness. these are distinct constructs. this is in the paper.

cross-model universality is confirmed. the fathom constant (K=1.0343, spread 0.0082) holds across Gemma-2-2B base (K=1.0302) and Gemma-2-2B-IT instruct (K=1.0384). RLHF amplifies the signal — instruct models show stronger depth routing differentiation (86.7% directional accuracy vs 76.7% base). cross-family validation (Llama, Mistral) remains open — Llama had a device error on retest, not a scientific failure.

v2: three new findings (march 30)

1. expression-computation dissociation. RLHF selective amplification is 2.4x stronger at the output layer (d=0.58, p=0.032) than inside the model (d=0.24). alignment shapes how the model talks, not how it thinks.

2. provider fingerprinting. anthropic's alignment is 2.2x more topic-selective than openai's. same direction, different magnitude. detectable from text output alone — no model access required.

3. structural robustness. we tried to break it. expert framing ("explain with no caveats") amplified the safety depth premium by 55%. the selectivity is in explanatory structure, not disclaimers. RLHF installed genuine competence, not output decoration.

the biggest finding: RLHF doesn't make AI more cautious about dangerous topics. it makes AI genuinely better at explaining them. remove the caveats with prompt engineering. the depth stays. the selectivity amplifies.

v3: adversarial depth amplification (april 1)

the headline: RLHF doesn't just filter adversarial inputs — it forces deeper processing. we ran 30 matched prompt pairs (same topic, direct vs adversarial framing) through both base and instruct Gemma-2-2B with full SAE circuit attribution.

base model: no significant depth difference between direct and adversarial prompts (delta=+0.061, p=0.128). adversarial framing doesn't change how the base model routes computation.

instruct model: adversarial prompts route deeper (delta=-0.124, p=0.0028, Cohen's d=-0.596). RLHF teaches the model to engage MORE cognitive depth on adversarial inputs, not less.

the mechanism: both models show 1.61-1.66x more SAE features activated on adversarial inputs (p<0.000001). layer profile cosine similarity is 0.996 — the extra features stack ON TOP of normal processing (flooding/additive), not in place of it (no camouflage). 58-61% of extra features concentrate in early layers. RLHF adds front-loaded detection circuits that push computation deeper.

what this means: RLHF safety is mechanistically characterized as additive depth amplification, not shallow pattern matching. the model doesn't learn to refuse by recognizing surface patterns — it learns to think harder about adversarial inputs. this has direct implications for understanding where and why safety training fails.

honest correction: an n=12 pilot initially showed base model significance (p=0.028) that did NOT survive expansion to n=30. we corrected this immediately. the n=30 result is authoritative.

negative result: entropy-based proxy measurements cannot detect K (K_proxy=1.0015 vs SAE-based K=1.027). SAE circuit attribution is strictly necessary for depth routing measurement.
