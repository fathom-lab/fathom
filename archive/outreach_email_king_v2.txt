Hi Jean-Remi,

Your TRIBE result (70x resolution, transformer representations predicting brain activity) raises an immediate question: *why* do some layers predict brain activity better than others?

We built the instrument that answers this.

Fathom uses SAE circuit attribution to map where computation concentrates across transformer layers during inference. We found that surface recall (factual retrieval) concentrates activation at output-proximal layers, while explanatory reasoning distributes activation across mid-layers. Pre-registered, n=30, p=0.000051, leave-one-out robust.

Here is what we can give you, for free, immediately:

1. Fathom depth scores for every layer of every model in your TRIBE pipeline — this tells you which layers are doing recall vs reasoning, which should predict which brain regions.

2. A testable prediction: your best-predicting layers for prefrontal cortex fMRI should be the same layers where Fathom detects distributed reasoning. Your best-predicting layers for hippocampal activity should be the output-proximal layers where Fathom detects recall.

3. We already ran a preliminary comparison. The activation topology converges: universality coefficient 0.830 across transformer and fMRI reference data. Same [early, mid, late] distribution structure, different substrates.

What we need from you (and only you have):

Individual-subject fMRI data task-matched to our 30 calibrated prompt pairs. TRIBE uses naturalistic stimuli — we would design a complementary protocol using vocabulary-controlled reasoning vs recall prompts. The combination would be the first mechanistic bridge between circuit-level AI computation and neural-level brain computation.

That paper goes to Nature Human Behaviour, not NeurIPS.
