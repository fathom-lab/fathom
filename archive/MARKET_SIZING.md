# FATHOM — Market Sizing Analysis
**AI Evaluation & Reasoning Intelligence Market**
*March 2026*

---

## Executive Summary

Fathom operates at the intersection of three converging markets: AI evaluation/observability, AI safety & interpretability, and enterprise AI compliance. The addressable opportunity is large, underpenetrated, and structurally similar to early application performance monitoring (APM) markets that generated $5B+ companies (Datadog, New Relic).

**TL;DR:**
- **TAM:** $50.4B (2030 projected)
- **SAM:** $8.2B (regulated enterprise AI eval)
- **SOM:** $82M (achievable by Year 3 via API + SaaS in 3 verticals)

---

## TAM — Total Addressable Market

### $50.4B by 2030

**Component 1: AI Evaluation & Testing Tools — $18B**
- The global AI testing and QA market was ~$6B in 2024
- Growing at ~24% CAGR driven by enterprise AI deployment mandates
- Includes: model benchmarking, red-teaming, output quality scoring, regression testing
- Source basis: Gartner AI Governance Market, IDC AI Lifecycle Management projections

**Component 2: AI Observability & Monitoring — $14B**
- Runtime monitoring of AI systems (latency, drift, hallucination rates)
- Growing fastest in regulated industries (healthcare, finance, legal)
- Current players: Weights & Biases, Arize AI, WhyLabs, Arthur AI
- None measure reasoning depth — they measure output behavior
- Fathom addresses the gap: *pre-output depth intelligence*

**Component 3: AI Compliance & Auditability — $12B**
- EU AI Act (effective 2025) requires explainability for high-risk AI systems
- US federal procurement now demanding AI transparency reporting
- Enterprise legal teams need audit trails for AI-assisted decisions
- Market growing at 31% CAGR as regulatory pressure accelerates

**Component 4: AI Safety Tooling — $6.4B**
- Interpretability tools, alignment testing, capability evaluation
- Primarily research-driven today — rapidly commercializing
- Fathom's SAE methodology places it directly in this category
- OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepMind all investing heavily in interpretability infrastructure

**Total TAM:** $50.4B (2030, conservative blended estimate)

## SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market

### $8.2B — Regulated Enterprise AI Evaluation

SAM narrows to enterprises in regulated verticals that are:
1. Actively deploying LLMs in production
2. Subject to explainability or compliance requirements
3. Procuring third-party AI evaluation tools

**Healthcare AI Evaluation — $2.1B**
- FDA AI/ML action plan requires performance monitoring of AI-assisted diagnostics
- Hospital systems deploying AI for clinical decision support need depth assurance
- ~8,000 hospital systems in US alone; growing internationally
- Average annual eval tooling spend: ~$250K/system for large deployments

**Financial Services AI Evaluation — $2.4B**
- OCC, Fed Reserve, CFPB all issuing AI model risk management guidance
- Banks and insurers require SR 11-7 model validation for AI systems
- Fathom depth scores = auditable reasoning evidence for model risk management
- ~5,000 large financial institutions globally; enterprise contracts $200K–$1M annually

**Legal Tech AI Evaluation — $1.6B**
- Law firms and legal AI vendors facing ABA ethics guidelines on AI use
- "Competent supervision" of AI requires knowing what it reasoned, not just what it said
- Fathom: first tool that can show depth of legal reasoning, not just accuracy

**Government / Defense AI Evaluation — $1.3B**
- DoD AI Ethics Principles require accountability and traceability
- Federal procurement moving toward AI transparency requirements
- Fathom certification = procurement differentiator for AI vendors selling to government

**Education Technology AI — $0.8B**
- Highest domain K (1.115) suggests most reasoning variation in education
- Personalized learning AI needs depth calibration (shallow vs. deep Socratic response)
- EdTech vendors + school district AI procurement

**Total SAM:** $8.2B

## Competitive Landscape

### Direct Competitors: None (Category Creation)

No existing tool measures reasoning depth via SAE circuit attribution. Fathom is creating a new instrument category.

### Adjacent Players (Partial Overlap)

| Company | What They Do | What They Miss |
|---|---|---|
| Arize AI | Output drift monitoring | No depth measurement |
| WhyLabs | Data quality + output monitoring | No circuit-level attribution |
| Arthur AI | Bias + fairness monitoring | No reasoning depth |
| Weights & Biases | Experiment tracking | Not an eval product |
| Scale AI RLHF | Human preference rating | Output-only, not process |
| Anthropic Interp | Internal SAE research | Not productized, not for sale |

**Fathom's moat:** The Fathom Constant. Once K is established as the industry standard for reasoning depth, every evaluation pipeline calibrates against it. That's a standards moat, not just a product moat.

## Market Timing

**Why now:**

1. **SAE interpretability matured in 2024–2025** — tools exist, methodology proven
2. **Enterprise AI deployment accelerating** — 67% of Fortune 500 now have AI in production (McKinsey 2025)
3. **Regulatory pressure landing** — EU AI Act effective, US guidance in motion
4. **Interpretability gap widening** — models getting more capable while explainability lags
5. **No incumbent owns "depth"** — the category is empty

The APM analogy: New Relic and Datadog entered a market where companies knew their apps were slow but couldn't measure why. They built the instruments. They became the standard. Fathom is doing the same for AI reasoning.

**The window:** SAE-based interpretability is 18 months old as a practical methodology. First mover who productizes the standard wins the category. That window is 12–24 months.
